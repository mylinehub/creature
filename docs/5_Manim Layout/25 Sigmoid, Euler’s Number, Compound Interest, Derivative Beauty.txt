PART 5
NOW DERIVATIVE OF SIGMOID
We start:
s(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}

REWRITE
(1+e^{-x})^{-1}

DIFFERENTIATE
Using chain rule:
s'(x)=e^{-x}(1+e^{-x})^{-2}

LOOKS UGLY.
But now beauty begins.

REMEMBER ORIGINAL SIGMOID
s(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}

REARRANGE
s(x)(1+e^{-x})=1

EXPAND
s(x)+s(x)e^{-x}=1

MOVE TERMS
s(x)e^{-x}=1-s(x)

SUBSTITUTE INTO DERIVATIVE
Derivative was:
s'(x)=e^{-x}s(x)^2

FACTOR
s(x)(s(x)e^{-x})

SUBSTITUTE
s(x)e^{-x}=1-s(x)

FINAL BEAUTY
s'(x)=s(x)(1-s(x))

HUGE REALIZATION
Derivative became:
expression entirely using sigmoid itself.
